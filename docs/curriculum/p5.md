# Part 5 — Calculus meets linear algebra

**Through-line.** Everything a learner needs to read a training loop. Slopes along <span class="c-i">î</span> and <span class="c-j">ĵ</span> assemble into one arrow (the gradient, p5-01). How those slopes change assembles into a symmetric matrix (the Hessian, p5-02), whose eigenvectors are the bowl's principal curvatures — so the stability limit η < 2/λmax of gradient descent (p5-03) is read off a picture the learner already owns from p4-06. Regularization is the same bowl meeting a norm ball from p1-05 (p5-04). A nonlinear map is locally a matrix (the Jacobian, p5-05), and chaining maps multiplies those matrices — backprop, with the transpose pull-back from p2-10 and rank-1 weight gradients (p5-06). Order is fixed: p5-01 → p5-02 → p5-03 → p5-04 → p5-05 → p5-06. Gradient, Hessian, gradient descent, Jacobian and chain rule are separate lessons on purpose; each introduces at most one new symbol per step.

**Part-level color roles (in addition to STYLE.md).** The gradient ∇f is the vector under study: yellow `c-v`. A probe or candidate direction u: purple `c-w`. A gradient-descent path and its step arrows: orange `c-b` (the "target/bias" hue is free in this part; the narration always says "the orange path"). Hessian eigenvectors / principal curvature axes / the minimum: gold `c-gold`. The stability limit 2/λmax: a red line drawn with the engine's `bad` token (narration says "the red line"; no color class). Data classes follow STYLE.md: "the blue points" / "the rose points". Shadows and pull-backs: magenta `c-mag` with dashed gray residuals. Contours: dim gray polylines; the contour through the current point: gold.

**Default functions (reused across the part).** Bowl `f(x,y) = x² + 4y²` (∇f = [2x; 8y], H = diag(2, 8)); rotated bowl `f = ½xᵀHx` with `H = [[5,3],[3,5]]` (λ = 8 along (1,1)/√2, λ = 2 along (1,−1)/√2); GD bowl `H = [[5.5,−4.5],[−4.5,5.5]]` (λ = 10 along (1,−1)/√2, λ = 1 along (1,1)/√2, κ = 10, 2/λmax = 0.2); bend map `F(x,y) = (x + 0.4y², y + 0.4x²)` (J = [[1, 0.8y],[0.8x, 1]], det J = 1 − 0.64xy). All contour maps use `g.contours` with the levels listed per lesson over `range [−3,3]²` unless stated; in 3D view contours are vertex-projected polylines on the z = 0 floor, never an image.

## p5-01-gradient — The gradient is a vector
**Part** 5 · **Tags** core · **Dims** both · **Minutes** ~10 · **Prereqs** p1-06-dot-product, p4-06-symmetric-quadratic-forms, p0-02-reading-the-symbols
**Goal.** The learner can read a contour map, build the gradient arrow from the two partial slopes, and knows it points uphill perpendicular to the contour — not at the minimum.
**Badge.** drawnIn: 2D contour map + 3D surface over two inputs · realSize: ∇L of a small CNN has ~10⁶ entries; of GPT-class models 10⁹–10¹² · transfers: one partial slope per axis, ∇f ⟂ level sets, steepest ascent along ∇f, f(p+d) ≈ f(p) + ∇f·d — exact in any n.
**Can't show.**
- Level sets in n inputs are (n−1)-dimensional sheets, not curves.
- A height surface exists only for two inputs; for n inputs there is no "hill" to draw.
- Kinks (ReLU) where the gradient is undefined; every f drawn here is smooth.
**Terms.** function of two variables — a rule that turns a point (x, y) into one number f(x, y); contour / level set — all points with the same value of f; partial derivative ∂f/∂x — the slope of f when only x moves; gradient ∇f — the vector [∂f/∂x; ∂f/∂y] of all partial slopes; directional derivative — the slope of f along a unit direction u, equal to ∇f·u; steepest ascent/descent — the directions ∇f and −∇f; tangent plane — the flat sheet that touches the surface at a point.
**State.** `s.fn` ('bowl'|'round'|'saddle'|'hills'); `s.p` [x,y] current point; `s.probe` angle θ of the purple unit direction u; `s.d` small displacement for the linear-approximation step; `s.showField`, `s.showTangent`, `s.showSlice` booleans; `s.levels` contour levels; `s.guess` [2] typed gradient (check 3); `s.strip` 12-cell gradient for the mlTie step; `s.view`, `s.cam`. Derived every frame via a pure helper `grad(s.fn, p)` (closed form per preset: bowl [2x, 8y]; round [2x, 2y]; saddle [2x, −2y]; hills = sum of two Gaussians 2·exp(−((x−1)²+(y−0.8)²)) + 1.5·exp(−((x+1.2)²+(y+0.6)²)), analytic gradient).
**Steps.**
### 1. Height is a function · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: A function <b>f</b> takes a point (<span class="c-i">x</span>, <span class="c-j">y</span>) and returns one number. Draw that number as height and you get a landscape. Here <b>f(x, y) = x² + 4y²</b>: a bowl, steeper along <span class="c-j">ĵ</span> than along <span class="c-i">î</span>.
Canvas: `s.view='3d'`, camera yaw 0→40° over t, pitch 35°. `g.surface(f, [−3,3]², {res:24, wire})` dim blue; `g.contours(f, levels [0.5,1,2,3,4,6,8,12], range)` as polylines on the floor; yellow `g.point` at p = (1, 0.5) on the floor with a dashed vertical `g.line` up to the surface at height 1.5; `g.label("f = 1.5")` at the top.
Math panel: f(x, y) = x² + 4y² · f(1, 0.5) = 1.5
### 2. The map · 2.5 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Look straight down and the landscape becomes a map. Each gray curve joins points of equal height: a <b>contour</b>. Close curves mean steep ground. The point sits on the contour f = 1.5, drawn gold.
Canvas: over t: camera pitch 35°→90° while the surface alpha fades 1→0; at t = 0.6 `s.view` switches to '2d' showing the same contours (`g.contours`) with small level labels; the contour through p is drawn gold (`g.polyline` of the level-1.5 curve); yellow point p.
Math panel: f = 1.5 on the gold curve
### 3. Slope along î · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Freeze <span class="c-j">y</span> and walk along <span class="c-i">î</span>: how fast does the height rise? That is the <b>partial derivative</b> ∂f/∂x. At our point it is 2: one step right climbs 2.
Canvas: 2D map; a green `g.vector` from p of length 0.4·2 along +x (scale 0.4 world units per slope unit); inset `g.lineChart` (bottom-right, 30% width) of the slice f(x, 0.5) for x∈[−3,3] with the tangent line at x = 1 drawn green, slope label 2; the slice point pulses.
Math panel: ∂f/∂x = 2x = <span class="c-i">2</span>
### 4. Slope along ĵ · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Now freeze <span class="c-i">x</span> and walk along <span class="c-j">ĵ</span>. The slice is a narrower parabola, so the slope is larger: ∂f/∂y = 8y = 4. Two directions, two slopes.
Canvas: red `g.vector` from p of length 0.4·4 along +y; inset lineChart switches to the slice f(1, y) with its red tangent at y = 0.5, slope label 4; both colored arrows remain.
Math panel: ∂f/∂y = 8y = <span class="c-j">4</span>
### 5. Two slopes make one arrow · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Put the two slopes into one bracket. That vector is the <b>gradient</b> <span class="c-v">∇f</span> = [2; 4]. It lives on the map, not on the hill: it is an arrow in the input plane, built from the green and red components.
Canvas: the green and red arrows slide tip-to-tail from p (red copy translated to the green tip, 0→0.5); the yellow `g.vector` ∇f = [2;4]·0.4 from p appears (0.5→1) with `components:'xy'`; numbers fly (`ctx.fx.fly`) from the arrow tips into the bracket.
Math panel: <span class="c-v">∇f</span> = [∂f/∂x; ∂f/∂y] = [<span class="c-i">2</span>; <span class="c-j">4</span>]
### 6. Perpendicular to the contour · 4 s · flags: (none)
Narration: The gradient is always at right angles to the contour through its point. Watch the point slide along the gold curve: the yellow arrow turns with it, never leaning along the curve. Along a contour the height does not change, so the climb must be straight across.
Canvas: p moves along the level-1.5 contour (parametrized by angle, t 0→1 one full loop); yellow ∇f recomputed each frame; dashed gray tangent segment at p; `g.arc(p, tangent, ∇f, {right:true})` right-angle marker.
Math panel: <span class="c-v">∇f</span> · (tangent) = 0
### 7. Steepest climb · 4 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Choose any unit direction <span class="c-w">u</span>. The slope along it is the dot product <span class="c-v">∇f</span>·<span class="c-w">u</span>, the shadow from p1-06. It is largest when u lines up with the gradient, zero across the contour, most negative straight downhill.
Canvas: p fixed at (1, 0.5); purple unit arrow u rotates θ 0→360° over t; magenta shadow of u onto the ∇f line with dashed residual; a single `g.bars` bar (screen inset) shows ∇f·u ∈ [−4.47, 4.47], green when positive, violet when negative; tick marks at θ where it crosses 0.
Math panel: slope along <span class="c-w">u</span> = <span class="c-v">∇f</span>·<span class="c-w">u</span> = ‖∇f‖ cos θ
### 8. The whole field · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Draw the gradient at every point: a field of arrows, each perpendicular to its contour, longer where the contours crowd together. The map and the field carry the same information.
Canvas: `g.field(grad, {range:[−3,3], step:0.5, scale:0.12})` arrows in dim yellow fade in 0→1; contours stay; the arrow at p drawn full yellow.
Math panel: ‖<span class="c-v">∇f</span>‖ = √((2x)² + (8y)²)
### 9. ✕ It does not point at the bottom · 4 s · flags: misconception
Narration: A common belief: "−∇f points at the minimum." Not here. At (2, 0.5) the downhill arrow aims 31° away from the center. The gradient only knows the slope under your feet; the bowl's shape decides where the bottom is. Next lesson measures that shape.
Canvas: p tweens to (2, 0.5); yellow −∇f = −[4; 4]·0.4; dashed gray arrow from p to the origin labeled "to the minimum"; `g.arc` between them labeled 31°; `g.marker.cross` on the gray label "−∇f points at the minimum"; a short orange step of length 0.3 along −∇f shows the fastest local drop while missing the center.
Math panel: −<span class="c-v">∇f</span> = [−4; −4] · direction to the minimum = [−2; −0.5]
### 10. Tangent plane and the first-order rule · 4 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Back in 3D: at any point the surface is almost flat — the <b>tangent plane</b>. Its tilt is the gradient. For a small displacement <span class="c-w">d</span>, the height changes by about <span class="c-v">∇f</span>·<span class="c-w">d</span>. This one line is what every training step uses.
Canvas: `s.view='3d'`, camera eases to yaw 30°, pitch 30°; surface wire; at p = (1, 0.5) a small `g.planes` square (side 1.2) tangent to the surface, teal alpha 0.3; the yellow ∇f arrow drawn on the floor; purple d on the floor (length 0.4) and its lifted image on the tangent plane; readout of true f(p+d) vs f(p) + ∇f·d.
Math panel: f(p + <span class="c-w">d</span>) ≈ f(p) + <span class="c-v">∇f</span>·<span class="c-w">d</span>
### 11. Why ML cares: the loss landscape · 4 s · flags: mlTie
Narration: Replace (x, y) by two weights (w₁, w₂) and f by a loss <b>L</b>. Training walks downhill: <b>w ← w − η<span class="c-v">∇L</span></b>, the teaser from p1-02. Real models have millions of weights, so ∇L is a long strip of slopes. The picture stops at 3. The formula does not.
Canvas: 2D map with axes relabeled w₁, w₂; orange point w, yellow ∇L, purple step −η∇L (η = 0.1) to the new point; below, a 12-cell strip (`g.heat2d` 1×12, diverging palette) of a seeded gradient `LA.rng(501)` labeled "∇L ∈ ℝ¹²"; caption text "The picture stops at 3. The formula does not."
Math panel: w ← w − η<span class="c-v">∇L</span>
### 12. Recap · 2 s · flags: recap
Narration: Three things to keep. The gradient collects one slope per axis into one arrow. It is perpendicular to the contour and points uphill fastest. It does not point at the minimum — only the bowl's shape knows that.
Canvas: three mini-cards: (1) green+red arrows → yellow arrow with bracket; (2) contour with ⟂ marker; (3) the 31° picture with the ✕.
Math panel: —
**Playground.** controls: preset · `fn` · bowl | round | saddle | hills · "Function"; slider · `probe` · 0–360 · "Probe direction θ"; toggle · `showField` · — · "Arrow field"; toggle · `view` · 2d/3d · "3D surface"; toggle · `showTangent` · — · "Tangent plane". **Checks.**

**ML tie-in.** The loss of any model is a function of its weights; the gradient is the vector of per-weight slopes that every optimizer consumes. This lesson gives the two facts the learner will reuse in p5-03 and p6-08: the gradient is perpendicular to loss contours, and it is a local measurement that does not know where the minimum is.
**Recap cards.** (1) "One slope per axis, one arrow" — green/red components → yellow ∇f. (2) "Perpendicular to contours, uphill" — contour with right-angle marker. (3) "Not a pointer to the minimum" — the 31° gap with the ✕.

## p5-02-hessian-curvature — The Hessian and curvature
**Part** 5 · **Tags** core · **Dims** both · **Minutes** ~10 · **Prereqs** p5-01-gradient, p4-06-symmetric-quadratic-forms, p4-02-eigenvectors
**Goal.** The learner can build the 2×2 Hessian from "slopes of slopes", recognizes a bowl as ½xᵀHx, and reads its eigenvectors as the directions of greatest and least curvature.
**Badge.** drawnIn: 2D contours + 3D surface over two inputs · realSize: the Hessian of a 10⁶-weight network is 10⁶×10⁶ — never stored, only products Hv · transfers: H is symmetric, uᵀHu is the curvature along u, eigenvectors are principal curvature directions, λmax sets the step limit — exact in any n.
**Can't show.**
- n inputs have n principal curvatures; we draw two.
- For non-quadratic f the Hessian changes from point to point; we draw quadratics where it is constant everywhere.
- Indefinite Hessians in n-D can bend down along many directions at once; the saddle shows one.
**Terms.** second derivative — the slope of the slope, how fast the slope changes as you move; mixed partial ∂²f/∂x∂y — how the x-slope changes when you move along y (always equals ∂²f/∂y∂x for smooth f); Hessian H — the symmetric matrix of all second derivatives; curvature along u — the second slope along a unit direction, uᵀHu; principal curvature directions — the eigenvectors of H, where curvature is largest and smallest; positive definite — all eigenvalues positive, a bowl; saddle — eigenvalues of both signs.
**State.** `s.H` 2×2 symmetric (edited via matrixInput with the lower-left cell mirrored); `s.p` [x,y]; `s.probe` angle of u; `s.showEig`, `s.showSlice` booleans; `s.view`, `s.cam`; `s.sliceDir` ('x'|'y'|'u') for the inset parabola; `s.eta` for the mlTie step; `s.guessH` 2×2 typed (check 1); `s.heat6` 6×6 synthetic symmetric matrix for the mlTie strip (seeded `LA.rng(502)`, A = BᵀB). f(x) = ½xᵀHx everywhere in this lesson; ∇f = Hx.
**Steps.**
### 1. The slope changes · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Last lesson the slope along <span class="c-i">î</span> at (1, 0.5) was 2. Move right and it grows: at x = 2 it is 4, at x = 3 it is 6. The slope itself has a slope — here 2 per unit of x. That number is the <b>second derivative</b>.
Canvas: 2D contours of f = x² + 4y² (levels as in p5-01); p slides from (−2, 0.5) to (2, 0.5) over t; green ∂f/∂x arrow length 0.4·2x grows linearly and flips sign at x = 0; inset `g.lineChart` of f(x, 0.5) with the tangent sliding; small readout "slope = 2x".
Math panel: ∂f/∂x = 2x → ∂²f/∂x² = <span class="c-i">2</span>
### 2. A steeper bend · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Along <span class="c-j">ĵ</span> the slope is 8y, so it changes four times faster: ∂²f/∂y² = 8. In the inset the y-parabola is narrower than the x-parabola. Curvature is "how fast the slope changes", one number per direction.
Canvas: p slides from (1, −1) to (1, 1); red arrow length 0.4·8y; inset shows both slices superimposed: wide green parabola f(x, 0.5), narrow red parabola f(1, y).
Math panel: ∂f/∂y = 8y → ∂²f/∂y² = <span class="c-j">8</span>
### 3. Does the x-slope care about y? · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Move along <span class="c-j">ĵ</span> and watch the green arrow: it does not change. For this bowl the x-slope ignores y, so the <b>mixed partial</b> ∂²f/∂x∂y is 0. Soon we meet a bowl where it is not.
Canvas: p slides from (1, −1) to (1, 1) again but the camera follows the green ∂f/∂x arrow (constant length 0.8); a gray "unchanged" tag; the red arrow is dimmed.
Math panel: ∂²f/∂x∂y = 0
### 4. The 2×2 box · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Four second derivatives fit a 2×2 box: the <b>Hessian</b> <b>H</b>. The two mixed partials are always equal, so H is symmetric — the nice case from p4-06. For our bowl H = [[2, 0],[0, 8]].
Canvas: the numbers 2, 0, 0, 8 fly from the inset labels into a bracket drawn in the math panel (`ctx.fx.fly`); the diagonal pulses; off-diagonals show the equality with a double arrow.
Math panel: H = [[∂²f/∂x², ∂²f/∂x∂y],[∂²f/∂y∂x, ∂²f/∂y²]] = [[<span class="c-i">2</span>, 0],[0, <span class="c-j">8</span>]]
### 5. A bowl is ½xᵀHx · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Check: ½xᵀHx = ½(2x² + 8y²) = x² + 4y². Our bowl is exactly the quadratic form from p4-06 with A = H. Near any minimum, every smooth loss looks like this; the Hessian is the bowl's shape.
Canvas: `s.view='3d'`; `g.surface` of ½xᵀHx with floor contours; the p4-06 ellipse (level 2) highlighted on the floor with its gold axes along î and ĵ; camera orbits yaw 20°→50°.
Math panel: f(x) = ½ xᵀ H x
### 6. A tilted bowl · 4 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Now H = [[5, 3],[3, 5]]. The contours tilt and the mixed partial is 3: moving along <span class="c-j">ĵ</span> now changes the <span class="c-i">î</span>-slope. The slope along î is 5x + 3y — it depends on both coordinates.
Canvas: back to 2D; `s.H` tweens entrywise [[2,0],[0,8]] → [[5,3],[3,5]] (contours of ½xᵀHx at levels [1,2,4,8,12] recomputed per frame); then p slides (1,−1)→(1,1) and the green arrow 0.4·(5x+3y) visibly grows.
Math panel: H = [[5, 3],[3, 5]] · ∂f/∂x = 5x + 3y · ∂²f/∂x∂y = 3
### 7. Principal directions · 4 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Where does the tilted bowl bend most? Along the eigenvectors of H (p4-02): gold q₁ = (1, 1)/√2 with λ₁ = 8 is the steep bend, gold q₂ = (1, −1)/√2 with λ₂ = 2 the gentle one. The ellipse axes from p4-06 are exactly these directions.
Canvas: `LA.num.eigSym(s.H)`; two gold `g.vector`s from the origin with labels λ = 8, λ = 2; the ellipse level 4 drawn with its axes coinciding; inset shows the two slices along q₁ (narrow) and q₂ (wide).
Math panel: H = QΛQᵀ · λ₁ = <span class="c-gold">8</span>, λ₂ = <span class="c-gold">2</span>
### 8. Curvature in any direction · 4 s · flags: (none)
Narration: For a unit direction <span class="c-w">u</span> the second slope is <b>uᵀHu</b> — the quadratic form evaluated on u. Rotate u: the bar peaks at 8 on the steep gold axis and bottoms out at 2 on the gentle one. Every direction's curvature sits between the two eigenvalues.
Canvas: purple unit u rotates 0→360°; inset bar of uᵀHu ∈ [2, 8] with gold ticks at 2 and 8; the inset slice parabola along u reshapes continuously.
Math panel: curvature along <span class="c-w">u</span> = <span class="c-w">u</span>ᵀH<span class="c-w">u</span>
### 9. ✕ Steep is not the same as curved · 4 s · flags: misconception
Narration: "A big gradient means a sharp bend." No. Far out along the gentle axis the gradient is long but the bend is still λ = 2; near the bottom on the steep axis the gradient is short and the bend is 8. For a quadratic, H is the same at every point while ∇f changes everywhere.
Canvas: two probes: A = 2·q₂ (gradient ‖Hx‖ = 4, long yellow arrow) and B = 0.3·q₁ (gradient 2.4, short); the same small inset parabola shape per probe shows curvatures 2 vs 8; `g.marker.cross` on the label "steep ⇒ curved".
Math panel: ∇f = Hx changes with x · H does not
### 10. A saddle · 3 s · flags: (none)
Narration: Flip one eigenvalue's sign: H = [[2, 0],[0, −2]]. The surface rises along <span class="c-i">î</span> and falls along <span class="c-j">ĵ</span>; contours become hyperbolas. A negative eigenvalue is a direction where the slope decreases as you go — the saddle from p4-06.
Canvas: `s.H` tweens to [[2,0],[0,−2]]; `s.view='3d'` surface morphs bowl → saddle; floor contours become hyperbolas (levels [−4,−2,−1,1,2,4]); gold axes with λ labels 2 and −2 (negative one drawn violet-tinted gold).
Math panel: λ₂ = −2 < 0 ⇒ saddle
### 11. Why ML cares: the safe step · 4 s · flags: mlTie
Narration: Near a minimum, L(w) ≈ L(w*) + ½(w − w*)ᵀH(w − w*). Along the steepest bend λmax a step η overshoots when η > 2/λmax. Next lesson shows it. Real Hessians are huge; we show one 6×6 as a heatmap. The picture stops at 3. The formula does not.
Canvas: left: the slice parabola along q₁ (curvature 8) with a point at a = 1; two trial steps from the 1D rule a ← a − η·8a: η = 0.1 lands at 0.2 (inside, green check), η = 0.3 lands at −1.4 (farther out, red ✕); a red tick labeled 2/λmax = 0.25 on a small η axis. Right: 6×6 symmetric heatmap `s.heat6` (diverging) with caption "The picture stops at 3. The formula does not."
Math panel: L(w) ≈ L(w*) + ½(w − w*)ᵀH(w − w*) · safe: η < 2/λmax
### 12. Recap · 2 s · flags: recap
Narration: Keep three pictures. Slopes of slopes fill a symmetric 2×2 box. A bowl is ½xᵀHx, so H is its shape. The eigenvectors of H are the directions of sharpest and gentlest bend, and the sharpest one limits your step.
Canvas: three mini-cards: (1) the 2×2 box with the two parabolas; (2) contours over ½xᵀHx; (3) gold eigen-axes with λ labels.
Math panel: —
**Playground.** controls: matrix · `H` · entries −10…10 · "Hessian H (symmetric)"; slider · `probe` · 0–360 · "Probe direction"; toggle · `showEig` · — · "Eigen-axes"; toggle · `view` · 2d/3d · "3D surface"; preset · — · diag(2,8) | rotated [[5,3],[3,5]] | saddle [[2,0],[0,−2]] | round I. Drag handle on p. Live: contours, gold axes with λ, uᵀHu bar, inset parabola, ∇f at p.

## p5-04-regularization-geometry — L1 vs L2: why one gives zeros
**Part** 5 · **Tags** capstone · **Dims** 2D · **Minutes** ~10 · **Prereqs** p1-05-norms-distance, p5-03-gradient-descent, p4-12-pseudoinverse-conditioning
**Goal.** The learner can explain, from the shape of a norm ball meeting loss contours, why L1 produces exact zeros and L2 shrinks smoothly, and can read a solution path as λ grows.
**Badge.** drawnIn: 2D (two weights) · realSize: lasso over 10⁴ gene features; weight decay on 10⁹ weights · transfers: solution = first contact between the loss contours and a norm ball; corners on the axes give exact zeros; ridge = (H + λI)⁻¹Hw* — exact in any n; the L1 ball in n-D has 2n corners, so the cartoon understates sparsity.
**Can't show.**

- Elastic net and group norms (one sentence in the recap only).
- Non-quadratic losses (logistic) whose contours are not ellipses.
**Terms.** regularization — adding a penalty on the size of w to the loss; norm ball — all w with ‖w‖ ≤ t (p1-05); L2 / ridge — penalty λ‖w‖₂², the disk; L1 / lasso — penalty λ‖w‖₁, the diamond; sparsity — some weights exactly zero; solution path — the curve of solutions as λ (or t) sweeps; weight decay — the L2 penalty written as a per-step shrink of w.
**State.** `s.H` 2×2 (default [[1, 0.5],[0.5, 1]]); `s.wstar` [2, 1] unconstrained minimum; `s.p` norm exponent (1 or 2 in steps; 0.5–3 in playground); `s.t` ball radius; `s.sol` current constrained solution; `s.pathL1`, `s.pathL2` arrays of solutions for t from 0 to ‖w*‖_p (60 samples); `s.lambda` for the penalty view; `s.showPaths`; `s.rho` off-diagonal of H (tilt); `s.guess` [2] typed ridge solution (check 3); `s.strip` 12-weight before/after vector for the mlTie step.
